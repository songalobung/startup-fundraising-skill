# Research references

Checked 2026-11-03. These references inform the skill's workflow. Live rules and program details can change; verify them directly before use.

## Multi-agent use

- OpenAI, [Multi-agent guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/responses-multi-agent): recommends subagents for concrete, independent workstreams and notes the added coordination and token cost when the work is sequential or small.
- OpenAI, [Skills](https://developers.openai.com/plugins/concepts/skills): describes a skill as reusable workflow instructions and distinguishes those instructions from the tools and runtime that provide live data, authentication, and actions. A skill may guide delegation where supported; it does not create subagents on its own.

## Financing choice and market evidence

- British Business Bank, [Everything you need to know about external finance](https://www.british-business-bank.co.uk/business-guidance/guidance-articles/finance/everything-you-need-to-know-about-external-finance): overview of equity and debt paths and factors in selecting finance. UK program availability is location-specific.
- U.S. Small Business Administration, [Market research and competitive analysis](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/market-research-competitive-analysis): guidance on demand, market size, customer research, and direct and indirect competition.

## Fundraising narrative and materials

- Y Combinator, [A Guide to Seed Fundraising](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-raise-a-seed-round/): practitioner guidance on conversations and fundraising materials. Its examples reflect one ecosystem; do not treat them as universal requirements.
- Y Combinator Startup Library, [How to talk to investors](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/dc-how-to-start-a-startup-sales-and-marketing-how-to-talk-to-investors): practitioner discussion of pitch conversations and traction.
- Y Combinator Startup Library, [How startup fundraising works](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/Il-how-startup-fundraising-works): founder-oriented discussion of communicating plainly and demonstrating progress.

## U.S. securities context

- U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, [Offering Pathways](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/capital-raising-building-blocks/offering-pathways): overview of U.S. registered and exempt offering pathways.
- SEC, [Exempt Offerings](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings): overview of common U.S. offering exemptions and filing context.
- SEC, [Private Companies and the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/capital-raising-building-blocks/private-companies-sec): explains that securities laws apply to private-company offers and sales and why counsel matters.
- SEC, [Frequently Asked Questions About Exempt Offerings](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/frequently-asked-questions-about-exempt-offerings): high-level U.S. exemption, antifraud, and state-law context.
- SEC, [What is Form D?](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/capital-raising-building-blocks/what-form-d): SEC small-business guidance on Form D and EDGAR process.

These are U.S.-specific sources. They do not determine requirements in other jurisdictions and are not legal advice. A fundraising email, pitch deck, social post, or other public statement can raise different legal issues depending on its facts, audience, and jurisdiction; the skill therefore directs founders to counsel instead of treating marketing as risk-free.
